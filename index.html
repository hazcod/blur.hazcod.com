Not an AI that reads everything

Most memory tools send your history to a model and let it work things out. Blur is designed the other way round.

  • AI is optional and off by default. Use a local Ollama model or an endpoint you configure. Sharing memory evidence with a remote endpoint needs a separate opt-in; the provider may retain requests.
  • A local planner reads your question, not a model. It is deterministic. Time questions and "what did you capture from WhatsApp" are answered with no model at all.
  • A model only gets a handful of items. They are selected and size-limited, each with its time and app, never your whole memory. Blur shows how many items were sent for each answer.
  • Recaps start from summaries. They are built from short hourly summaries Blur already wrote, so a recap of today is a few kilobytes, not hundreds of captures.
  • You stay in control. You decide what is captured with allowed and blocked apps and sites and timed pauses, how long it is kept, and you can delete all of it.

How it works

Capture and search stay on your device. AI is there when you choose to use it.

  1. Capture

    Blur reads accessible text from the focused app: Accessibility on macOS, UI Automation on Windows, and AT-SPI2 on Linux. It never takes screenshots.

  2. Encrypted store

    Captured text goes into a local encrypted database. The key lives on your device.

  3. Ask or recap

    The LLM is only used when necessary, and it only sees the few items a local planner picked for your question. Answers cite what they came from.

Private by default

You decide what is captured, how long it is kept and which optional connections can share it.

  • Stored locally and encrypted. Your database key is protected by macOS Keychain, Windows DPAPI or Linux Secret Service. The macOS app also uses App Sandbox.
  • Text capture, without screenshots. Blur uses each platform's accessibility APIs and follows your explicit start and pause controls. Some apps do not expose accessible text.
  • You choose what it sees. Pick the apps and sites it may capture and the ones it must never capture. Pause any time, including for 15 minutes, 1 hour or until tomorrow morning.
  • AI is optional and off by default. Use a local Ollama model or an OpenAI-compatible endpoint you configure. With a model on, you get hourly recaps and a daily recap. Without one, you get a model-free "This hour so far" draft.
  • Sync without a shared database. Blur writes encrypted, append-only segments, protected by a passphrase, to a folder you pick. The database is never synced, and deletions propagate. Your sync provider can still see file sizes and timing.
  • Retention is yours. Keep 30, 90 or 180 days, 1 year, or turn it off. "Delete all memory" removes everything.

For developers

Blur is written in Go, with a shared encrypted memory core and native platform adapters. Shipped targets build without cgo. Help validate Windows and Linux on real desktops, improve capture, or contribute to the shared core.
